# Deterministic Change Descriptors for Factual Revision Flagging: An Overlap-Audited VitaminC Study

**Authors:** R. M. N. Sai Prabhas, Pavanputra Charry (equal contribution)  
**Affiliation:** Department of Computer Science and Engineering, CMR Institute of Technology, Hyderabad  
**Project:** Global Misinformation Evolution Engine (GMEE)  
**Date:** 20 October 2026 - working manuscript  
**Scope:** Binary flagging of factual sentence revisions in the released VitaminC Wikipedia candidate pool.


## Abstract

Factual-revision flagging asks whether a before/after sentence edit changes a fact that could affect a claim. We test whether deterministic text-change descriptors add ranking information to a small lexical classifier on the human-reviewed VitaminC flagging release. Two primary L2 logistic models use the same ordered raw sentence pair; the augmented model adds 25 rule-based descriptors to eight lexical edit features. We fit on the full official training split, select hyperparameters on development rows remaining after overlap exclusions, and perform the original test evaluation under a local analysis freeze. The primary test subset excludes 220 of 31,048 rows with a normalized page title, revision identifier, or exact unordered sentence pair shared with training or development. On the remaining 30,828 rows, AUROC rises from 0.7225 to 0.8116, a paired difference of 0.0891 (95% page-group bootstrap interval [0.0761, 0.1020]; 2,000 draws). A connected-component sensitivity gives [0.0756, 0.1024]. Separately frozen post-primary comparisons score 0.8205 for a sparse edit TF-IDF model and 0.9189 for the authors' released ALBERT checkpoint on the same rows. An exploratory follow-up adds the descriptors to sparse text plus lexical features, increasing AUROC from 0.8317 to 0.8569 (descriptive page-group interval for the gain [0.0213, 0.0292]). The descriptors show complementary predictive information under this recipe, while remaining below the released checkpoint. These results do not validate individual semantic rules or natural-news claim evolution.

## 1. Introduction

A revision can change few words yet alter the proposition expressed by a sentence. Conversely, many visible edits leave the factual content intact. VitaminC defines a binary flagging task on before/after Wikipedia sentences: a positive revision changes the relation of at least one possible claim to the sentence [@schuster-etal-2021-get]. The task offers human-reviewed labels for a narrow question about textual change. It is distinct from verification of a supplied claim and from tracing whether information propagated between news outlets.

This study tests a limited hypothesis: when the classifier and raw sentence inputs are held constant, do deterministic text-change descriptors improve ranking beyond elementary lexical edit measurements? The descriptors come from an existing GMEE analyzer; they include typed change indicators and counts, similarity, quantity equivalence, and edit-span counts. We make no claim that this combination is a new model architecture or that the named types are validated human explanations. The useful research outcome would be a reproducible assessment of a compact feature representation, accompanied by a direct account of split contamination and dependence.

Our primary comparison finds a positive AUROC difference on a predeclared clean subset of the released VitaminC test split. Subsequent same-row sparse edit TF-IDF and released ALBERT checkpoint comparisons score higher than the augmented model. The authors' original paper reported AUC 79.87 for an edited-word bag-of-words MLP and 91.97 for a full-sentence ALBERT model on its published evaluation [@schuster-etal-2021-get]. Those published values use a different evaluation protocol and are not matched estimates for our clean subset; our pinned-checkpoint result supplies a same-row descriptive reference without retraining the author model. We claim neither state-of-the-art performance nor superiority to learned text encoders. A fixed qualitative error sample has been inspected but does not provide new human gold.

The paper's potential contribution is specific: a transparent, low-dimensional component comparison on real sentence revisions, stronger same-row text-model counterpoints, and a byte-pinned, input-defined leakage audit with paired dependence analysis. The sparse and ALBERT results narrow the method claim. An explicitly exploratory feature analysis further asks whether the descriptors complement sparse edit text rather than merely replace an elementary baseline. The study does not test whether GMEE correctly links cross-outlet claims, detects event evolution, extracts human-approved typed spans, handles correction context, determines truth, or traces copying and causal influence.

## 2. Related work

VitaminC mines Wikipedia revisions and supplies a factual-revision flagging task alongside contrastive claim–evidence verification and other tasks [@schuster-etal-2021-get]. Its flagging labels are the target here. We do not use the separate `vitaminc_real` claim–evidence benchmark as though it were a revision-label set. Our lexical and augmented logistic models are deliberately smaller than the paper's bag-of-words MLP and ALBERT baselines. The matched comparison isolates the addition of the fixed descriptors to one small classifier; it does not establish that the representation is the best available for the task.

Explicit revision-diff features and edit-intention taxonomies predate this study. Yang et al. classified 13 semantic intentions of Wikipedia revisions using, among other inputs, engineered revision-diff features [@yang-etal-2017-identifying-semantic]. Their article-level multi-label intention task differs from our sentence-pair binary target, but rules out a novelty claim for engineered differences or typed edit categories themselves. A recent survey places revision datasets, taxonomies, identification methods, and applications in a broader research line [@lan-etal-2026-making].

ClaimDiff concerns directional strengthening and weakening between claims from news articles on contentious issues, with human rationales [@ko-etal-2023-claimdiff]. Its claim-pair relations are useful motivation for comparing subtle statements, but they are not VitaminC's binary before/after factual-revision labels. *When Claims Evolve* studies robustness of embedding-based fact-check retrieval to edited misinformation claims [@magomere-etal-2025-claims]. It addresses an important neighboring problem in an end-to-end retrieval setting, whereas the present classifier is given an ordered sentence revision and does not retrieve a prior claim or evidence. NewsEdits 2.0 annotates intentions behind revisions of news articles, separating factual from stylistic and narrative changes [@spangher-etal-2024-newsedits]. That natural-news domain is closer to the broader application motivating GMEE; this study has not evaluated its human annotations or any cross-outlet news task.

## 3. Data and target

We use the authors' public `vitaminc_flagging.zip` release, pinned by the archive digest in Appendix A. The released JSONL contains 236,998 train, 36,624 development, and 31,048 test rows. The paper reports 304,671 reviewed revisions, while these three files contain 304,670 rows in total; our results refer to the downloaded, hashed bytes. Each model sees only `sent_a` and `sent_b` as ordered raw strings. The target is the release's `factual` versus `not factual` label. Neither released diff fields nor page title, revision ID, case ID, or label enters the predictors. Page and revision metadata are used only for exclusion and uncertainty grouping.

The dataset authors first used a BERT classifier to select approximately 305,000 candidate edits from a much larger Wikipedia revision pool, then asked trained annotators to mark factual changes [@schuster-etal-2021-get]. Thus the release is a **model-preselected candidate pool**, not a probability sample of Wikipedia changes. The source paper describes human review but does not report a flagging-specific inter-annotator agreement statistic. Its reported four-rater agreement check is for a different claim–evidence annotation task. We neither created nor claim independent human labels in this work.

The released split assignments were retained. An input-only audit found cross-split recurrence of exact unordered sentence pairs and revision IDs, despite near-disjoint page titles. We quarantined 234 development rows that match any full training row on a normalized page title, nonempty revision ID, or exact unordered raw sentence pair. The clean development set has 36,390 rows. We then predeclared the same rule for test rows against the **full** training and development files; 220 test rows were excluded, leaving 30,828 for the primary analysis (19,535 `not factual`, 11,293 `factual`). The full 31,048-row release is a secondary, overlap-disclosed population; we do not use it to revise the primary result.

The page key trims whitespace, replaces underscores with spaces, and casefolds the title. The unordered pair key uses both exact raw sentence strings in either order, with no sentence normalization. This is a conservative overlap and grouping choice: it can merge examples whose before/after sentence order differs, while the model is given the ordered before/after input. A post-score input audit found 27,456 unique unordered pairs and 28,590 unique **ordered** pairs among the 30,828 clean rows. It found no conflicting labels for identical ordered sentence inputs; 438 unordered groups containing 953 rows had both labels across reversed orders. This establishes a conflict under an unordered representation; reversal alone does not establish its cause or whether either label is wrong. We did not adjudicate these cases. These checks cannot catch paraphrases, all page aliases, or shared real-world events. The clean subset should therefore not be called independent by event or proposition.

## 4. Methods and local analysis freeze

### 4.1 Predictors

The lexical model contains eight features: normalized character Levenshtein distance; token-set Jaccard similarity; both character lengths and their absolute difference; and token insertion, deletion, and replacement counts from an untyped sequence diff. Tokens are extracted and casefolded by a deterministic regular expression.

The augmented model concatenates those eight features with 25 deterministic outputs of the frozen `gmee-typed-text-v2` text-change analyzer: similarity, meaningful-change and quantity-equivalence flags; one indicator and one count for each of nine analyzer mutation categories (`NUMERIC_DRIFT`, `ENTITY_SUBSTITUTION`, `POLARITY_SHIFT`, `HEDGING_SHIFT`, `ATTRIBUTION_SHIFT`, `SCOPE_SHIFT`, `FRAMING_SHIFT`, `WORDING_DRIFT`, `NEAR_DUPLICATE`); and total changed-span and insertion, deletion, and replacement span counts. These are algorithm-generated descriptors, not independently annotated change types. Some overlap conceptually with lexical features; the `FRAMING_SHIFT` and `NEAR_DUPLICATE` **count** fields are structurally zero in this implementation but remain in the frozen feature order for reproducibility. Any observed gain belongs to the **combined 33-feature implementation**, not specifically to quantity reasoning, a particular mutation type, or the analyzer as a full system.

For each feature set, we fit `StandardScaler` on training rows and then L2 logistic regression (`liblinear`, `max_iter=1000`, seed 1729). We use the full 236,998-row training split. The only regularization candidates are C = 0.01, 0.1, 1, and 10; selection maximizes clean-development AUROC, with a fixed smaller-C tie rule. The saved fit selected C = 10 for lexical and C = 0.01 for augmented. Secondary binary metrics use thresholds selected separately on clean development by maximizing macro-F1 over 0.00 through 1.00 in increments of 0.01; ties prefer the higher threshold. The selected thresholds are 0.31 and 0.38, respectively. Test labels did not determine feature construction, hyperparameters, or thresholds.

### 4.2 Estimand, uncertainty, and decision rule

The single primary endpoint is row-weighted AUROC on the clean test rows. The primary paired contrast is AUROC(augmented) minus AUROC(lexical), evaluated on identical rows. The local plan predeclared a +0.02 AUROC study margin, requiring the complete 95% interval above that value and at least 95% valid bootstrap draws. This is a study decision criterion; we did not establish its downstream operational utility.

The primary interval is a paired, two-sided 95% percentile bootstrap of the AUROC difference, resampling normalized page-title groups with replacement. Both models receive the same group draw and multiplicities; rows remain equally weighted within each draw. We requested 2,000 draws with seed 20261019, all of which were defined. A predeclared sensitivity repeats the paired bootstrap over input-defined connected components formed by shared normalized page hash, nonempty revision ID, or exact unordered raw pair hash. This second interval probes dependence missed by page grouping; it is descriptive and does not replace the primary estimand. We do not report a p-value or treat the secondary metrics as additional confirmatory tests.

### 4.3 Supplementary sparse-text comparison

After the primary two-model result was known, we specified and froze a supplementary same-row text baseline before running its test inference. It uses only the two raw sentences, aligns lowercased word tokens with a deterministic sequence diff, and marks removed and added tokens by old/new side. The resulting edit text enters a TF-IDF vectorizer with word unigrams and bigrams, training-only vocabulary and IDF, minimum document frequency 3, a maximum of 100,000 features, sublinear term frequency, and L2 row normalization. The fitted vocabulary contains 90,341 terms. We fit `liblinear` L2 logistic regression on the same full training rows, select C from {0.1, 1, 10} by clean-development AUROC (selected C = 1), and select a descriptive clean-development macro-F1 threshold (0.37). The baseline is not the VitaminC authors' neural bag-of-words model. Its timing and accuracy are descriptive; the post-primary comparison does not create a second confirmatory claim or alter the original material-gain decision rule. The supplementary protocol and source hashes are documented in `docs/VITAMINC_TEXT_BASELINE_SUPPLEMENT.md`.

### 4.4 Supplementary released-checkpoint comparison

After the primary and sparse scores were known, we separately froze inference with the VitaminC authors' released `tals/albert-base-vitaminc_flagging` checkpoint at revision `1e5f38d76c4d9402bf0c7d73e1aab6eaafca0ea8`. The checkpoint was trained by the dataset authors; we did not retrain, choose among checkpoints, calibrate, or tune a threshold. Its input is the same ordered raw `sent_a` and `sent_b` pair, tokenized to a maximum of 256 tokens with longest-first truncation and max-length padding. The positive score is softmax probability for class `factual`; descriptive classification metrics use a fixed threshold of 0.5. The original checkpoint run scored all 31,048 official test rows once; we report the original 30,828-row primary-eligible subset separately. The locally pinned run used float32 on an RTX 4060 GPU, batch size 32, PyTorch 2.5.1+cu121, and Transformers 4.46.3. It differs from the authors' historical fp16 recipe and is a fixed-checkpoint evaluation, not a bitwise reproduction of their published result. The plan and checkpoint hashes are in `docs/VITAMINC_ANCHOR_SUPPLEMENT.md` and the local lock. This is a post-primary descriptive reference, not a new confirmatory endpoint.

### 4.5 Exploratory feature partitions and sparse complement

After all four preceding test outcomes were known, we fixed a four-model feature analysis. Model A contains the eight lexical features plus analyzer similarity and the four changed/insert/delete/replace span counts (13 columns). Model B contains the eight lexical features plus the other 20 descriptors (28 columns). A is not a pure untyped control: its summaries come from the same analyzer and can reflect typed rules. Model C concatenates the sparse model's frozen, training-fitted TF-IDF representation with the eight lexical features. Model D uses the same sparse representation with all 33 dense features. StandardScaler is fitted only on training dense columns; the existing L2-normalized sparse rows are left unchanged and the concatenation is not normalized again.

All four use full training and the same clean development population, with L2 liblinear logistic regression, seed 1729, maximum 1,000 iterations, and C in {0.01, 0.1, 1, 10}. Development AUROC selects C using the original tie rule. Selected values are 0.01 for A/B and 1 for C/D; development-selected macro-F1 thresholds are 0.36, 0.40, 0.34, and 0.39. All model states, source dependencies, development outputs, and earlier artifact hashes were frozen before this supplement's original test run. Reloading models reproduced all development probabilities exactly.

We fixed two descriptive page-group contrasts: the saved original 33-feature model minus A, and D minus C, each using 2,000 paired draws and seed 20261019. Every model and contrast is reported. Because the analysis was outcome-informed, its intervals describe these comparisons without establishing new confirmatory discoveries. Correlated feature partitions cannot establish individual rule validity or causal semantic mechanisms.

### 4.6 Provenance and access disclosure

The protocol was a **local, content-hashed pre-test analysis freeze**, not an externally registered preregistration. It followed exploratory train/development fitting. Before the local freeze, a design audit exposed one **aggregate summary of test labels**. No row-level test labels, examples, predictions, or model outcomes were inspected at that time. The primary contrast had already been proposed, but this access weakens any claim that test information was perfectly sealed. The original scorer recorded a test-access marker and uses exclusive file creation to prevent repeated scoring from the same lock. The original evaluation completed once under that plan; the separately identified artifact reconstruction in Section 7 repeated computation after the results were known.

The primary and supplementary plans, saved model states, and result files are identified by content hashes (Appendix A). The locks preserve exact source copies, runtime versions, feature orders, data identities, and access markers. Row-level outputs remain local; the reproduction guide describes the public input sources and commands.

The released-checkpoint lock binds the same test bytes and original primary artifacts. It was created after the earlier results and does not amend their freeze.

**Table 1.** Scores on the predeclared clean subset. Only augmented versus lexical was the locally locked primary contrast. Their AUROC difference is 0.0891243913; its paired page-group 95% percentile interval is [0.0760953865, 0.1019605615] over 4,196 page groups and 2,000 valid draws. The interval lies above the predeclared +0.02 study margin under the local decision rule. Sparse and ALBERT results and all non-AUROC metrics are descriptive. The logistic thresholds (0.31 lexical, 0.38 augmented, 0.37 sparse) were selected on clean development; the ALBERT threshold was fixed at 0.5 before its test inference.

The connected-component sensitivity interval for the same AUROC contrast is [0.0756004715, 0.1024241337] over 4,192 input-defined components and 2,000 valid draws. The largest component contains 598 rows. There are 3,243 multirow components, containing 29,879 of the 30,828 clean rows. The contrast remains positive under these two group-resampling schemes, but the concentration of repeated or linked rows remains material. Neither interval covers unknown event-level dependence or bias from candidate selection.

The supplementary sparse model scores 0.8204875097 AUROC, 0.0088633563 above the augmented model. A descriptive paired page-group 95% interval for augmented minus sparse is [-0.0180922303, -0.0006181117] across 2,000 valid draws. The plan for this baseline was fixed **after** viewing the primary result but before its own test score, so this interval is not another preregistered discovery. Its better AUROC, average precision, macro-F1, and Brier score directly constrain the interpretation of the primary gain. The augmented model has a slightly lower log loss on these rows; no single secondary metric reverses the sparse model's AUROC ordering.

The released ALBERT checkpoint scores 0.9189335550 AUROC on the same clean rows, with average precision 0.8510, macro-F1 0.8317 at the fixed 0.5 threshold, Brier score 0.1110, and log loss 0.3414. Its confusion matrix (gold rows, predicted columns; `not factual`, `factual`) is [[16,042, 3,493], [1,484, 9,809]]. Its AUROC on all 31,048 official test rows is 0.9191; that full-release result includes the 220 known cross-split overlaps. The same-row scores show a substantial descriptive performance gap over the augmented model, but no paired uncertainty interval for that comparison was frozen. The ALBERT tokenization truncated 295 clean pairs at 256 tokens. The recorded 374.2-second GPU run includes a token-length audit and excludes setup and warm-up, so it is not a matched efficiency comparison with the logistic models.

The authors' published Table 3 reports AUC 0.7987 for its edited-word bag-of-words MLP and 0.9197 for ALBERT with full sentences [@schuster-etal-2021-get]. Our 0.9189 comes from the pinned released checkpoint on a different, overlap-filtered subset using a later float32 runtime; it is not a reproduction of the published 0.9197. The published bag-of-words and ALBERT numbers remain context rather than matched comparisons. The same-row checkpoint scores are higher than those of the evaluated compact feature model on AUROC and the listed secondary metrics.

### 5.2 Qualitative error hypotheses

We also inspected a seeded sample of 24 augmented-model errors, selecting 12 false positives and 12 false negatives across distinct normalized pages within each stratum. The review was AI-assisted, with no external factual adjudication or new human labels; it cannot estimate error-category prevalence. Recurring plausible failure modes included number words and date formats that the parser does not normalize, subtle role/scope changes or list deletions within otherwise similar sentences, and citation or category markup that creates misleading surface differences. In some examples, the isolated pair was insufficient to interpret the released label confidently. These observations are hypotheses for future contextual human review, not evidence that the release labels are wrong. The selection rule, sample hash, and text-free notes are in `docs/VITAMINC_QUALITATIVE_ERROR_REVIEW.md`.

## 6. Discussion and limitations

The result answers a narrow incremental question: combined deterministic change descriptors improve ranking over the declared lexical logistic baseline on the released VitaminC candidate pool. The post-primary sparse baseline's higher AUROC and the checkpoint's much higher AUROC show that this implementation is **not** the strongest of the same-row approaches evaluated here. A gain over eight lexical summaries is useful as a component check, but it does not show that hand-coded semantics outperform richer learned text representations. The exploratory analysis narrows this uncertainty: the descriptors add information beyond the selected similarity/span summaries, and their combination with sparse text improves over sparse text plus lexical features. This is evidence about predictive feature groups in this dataset. It does not isolate numeric, polarity, entity, or other individual contributions, validate their human-interpreted meanings, or establish a new confirmatory result. Coefficients alone would not establish those claims either.

The target is a human-reviewed, binary revision label. We did not obtain fresh adjudicated annotation or a flagging-specific inter-annotator reliability estimate. The selected candidate pool, Wikipedia domain, common quantitative edits, and lack of event-disjoint testing limit external validity. Excluding exact cross-split overlaps addresses a concrete leakage route, not all near-duplicate or historical dependence. More than 96% of clean test rows belong to nontrivial input-defined components, so a row count should not be mistaken for an independent unit count.

This experiment does not evaluate the broader GMEE task. The local news-pair corpus currently lacks two independent authenticated human labels per eligible pair and frozen event-disjoint splits. Assistant-generated labels cannot substitute for those people. We consequently do not claim validated news claim relatedness, temporal or provenance reasoning, changed-span accuracy, correction handling, source copying, or causal propagation. ClaimDiff and NewsEdits 2.0 motivate possible later external tasks, but their labels require separate task definitions and cannot be renamed into GMEE gold.

A separate read-only artifact review recomputed aggregate metrics from saved predictions and checked file and row identities without redrawing the bootstrap. The primary run used a Debian container on WSL2, Python 3.11.2, NumPy 2.2.6, SciPy 1.15.3, scikit-learn 1.7.2, and RapidFuzz 3.14.3. The sparse and feature-analysis runs used Windows Python 3.14.6 and scikit-learn 1.9.1; ALBERT used its pinned Windows GPU environment. Each lock retains actual source hashes and runtime versions because the research code was uncommitted.

On 20 October 2026, a same-team reconstruction extracted the original review bundle, verified all 64 payload entries, and installed three fresh environments matching the recorded package inventories. It refitted the seven classical models from verified training/development data and repeated inference with the released checkpoint. All eight models' test prediction files and all three development prediction files were byte-identical to their originals. The comparison passed 1,491 checks, including identities, exclusions, feature/vocabulary order, selections, fitted values, clean/full metrics, all reported bootstrap intervals, and artifact hash chains; every measured numerical difference was zero. The explicit mechanism adapter changed only predecessor lock identities and recorded its provenance. The receipt and comparison plan are included in the review package (Appendix A).

This reconstruction reused already acquired data and checkpoint bytes on the recorded hardware. All original outcomes were known; inherited historical disclosure text in the fresh primary lock is explicitly corrected by the reconstruction receipt. The package contains selected source, aggregate reports, environment inventories and instructions; no benchmark sentence text is reproduced here.

## 8. Conclusion and remaining submission gates

On a locally frozen, overlap-filtered VitaminC factual-revision test, adding deterministic text-change descriptors to a matched lexical logistic model improved AUROC by 0.0891, with a paired page-group interval [0.0761, 0.1020]. Separately frozen post-primary same-row comparisons scored 0.8205 AUROC for sparse edit text and 0.9189 for the authors' released ALBERT checkpoint, both above the augmented model's 0.8116. The primary finding is a component gain over lexical summaries. The exploratory sparse-complement comparison further improves AUROC from 0.8317 to 0.8569, suggesting that the descriptor group provides information beyond this sparse-plus-lexical recipe. The released checkpoint remains stronger. A fresh evaluation source is needed to confirm that complementary value; individual rule meanings and contextual error causes remain unvalidated. Validation of the larger news claim-evolution project additionally requires independent human annotation and event-disjoint evaluation; this paper should stay within the component scope unless that evidence is obtained.
